      About this simulator — the techniques, sizing, the model, and references

      Why bifurcation stenting is hard to picture

      Where a side branch leaves a coronary artery, a stent cannot simply be laid across the lesion: its struts cover the side-branch ostium, the carina can shift into the side branch, and every balloon that follows reshapes the metal. Each technique, from provisional stenting to the two-stent strategies, is a different sequence of wires, balloons and stents, and each leaves a different arrangement of metal behind. This page plays each sequence step by step on one LAD and first diagonal bifurcation, cut open so every strut and the carina can be seen, beside the same moment on fluoroscopy, and Compare sets two results side by side.

      Provisional stenting, and when two stents are planned

      The European Bifurcation Club's (EBC) 16th consensus sets out provisional stenting step by step: one stent in the main vessel across the side branch, a proximal optimisation (POT), after which the procedure may end, and further steps for the side branch only if it needs them. The same consensus puts the need for a second stent after provisional at 5 to 25 % of procedures; in the provisional group of the EBC TWO trial it was 16 %. The 18th consensus lists the reasons to stent the side branch after provisional: impaired flow, a stenosis above 70 % with angina or ECG changes, or a dissection beyond type B.

      The DEFINITION criteria call a bifurcation outside the left main complex when the side branch has a stenosis of at least 90 % over at least 10 mm (the major criterion) and at least two minor criteria are present: moderate or severe calcium, multiple lesions, an angle under 45° or over 70°, a main-vessel reference under 2.5 mm, thrombus, or a main-vessel lesion of 25 mm or more. The EBC reads DEFINITION II as favouring an upfront two-stent strategy in complex true bifurcations with a side-branch lesion longer than 10 mm; with shorter side-branch lesions provisional remains appropriate. The lesion on this page, a 90 % diagonal lesion over 10 mm with none of the minor criteria, meets the major criterion only, so by DEFINITION it is not complex and provisional would be the strategy. It is a teaching case: every technique runs on the same anatomy so their results can be compared like for like.

      Provisional stenting

      One stent sized 1:1 to the distal main vessel crosses the side branch, with the side-branch wire jailed, and leaves at least the length of the shortest POT balloon (6 to 8 mm) above the carina. The POT balloon is sized 1:1 to the proximal main vessel with its distal shoulder at the carina; on the bench a shoulder exactly at the carina cut plane did better than one 1 mm before or past it (Dérimay 2019). If the side branch needs more, it is rewired through a distal cell, next to the carina, opened by kissing balloons, and the POT is repeated. On a bench model, kissing alone left the proximal main vessel with an ellipticity index of 1.23, against 1.04 with POT, side-branch dilation and a final POT (Finet 2015).

      T and TAP

      When the side branch needs a stent after provisional (this page bails out for a dissection), the stent goes in through the main-vessel stent's opened distal cell. A T places it at the ostium with no protrusion, which the EBC finds feasible when the angle is close to 90°; in a micro-CT bench study, angles under 70° left a gap in the scaffolding at the side branch's take-off with a T (Foin 2013). TAP (T and small protrusion) covers the upper edge of the ostium with a protrusion described as 1 to 2 mm or less, then kisses the side-branch stent's balloon with a balloon in the main vessel. It leaves a single layer of metal as a new carina, 2.7 ± 1.4 mm long on IVUS (Hahn 2009, as cited by Burzotta 2015). At this page's 50° angle the model's TAP stent protrudes 2.3 mm into the LAD, measured along the LAD from the carina, the least that covers the diagonal's upper ostial edge. A plain T, placed with no protrusion, leaves the first 1.8 mm of the diagonal's wall past that edge bare, measured along the diagonal. Compare shows the two side by side.

      DK crush

      In the EBC's ten steps, the side-branch stent is placed with a protrusion of 2 to 3 mm into the main vessel (Chen's group describes 1 to 2 mm) and deployed beside a balloon parked in the main vessel. Its balloon and wire come out, the parked balloon and a POT crush the protrusion, the side branch is rewired through a proximal cell and a first kiss clears the crushed struts from the ostium. Then the main-vessel stent, a POT, a second rewire through a central cell, a final kiss and a final POT: two kisses, three POTs, and the last wire crosses one layer of metal. In DKCRUSH-1, final kissing was achieved in 100 % of DK crush procedures against 76 % of classic crush.

      Mini-crush

      Classic crush placed the side-branch stent's proximal marker 4 to 5 mm into the main vessel and crushed the protrusion with the main-vessel stent (Colombo 2003). Mini-crush keeps the protrusion to 1 to 2 mm proximal to the carina (Galassi 2008). Both stents go in together through a 7F guide (in 6F a balloon does the crush), the side-branch wire comes out before the main-vessel stent is deployed, and there is one kiss, so the last wire crosses the main-vessel stent and the crushed struts behind it. On a micro-CT bench, residual side-branch ostial stenosis was 36 % after mini-crush against 47 % after classic crush (Ormiston 2008).

      Culotte

      The EBC recommends the inverted culotte: the side-branch stent first, from the proximal main vessel into the side branch, a POT, the distal main vessel rewired through a distal cell and opened, then the main-vessel stent through that cell, overlapping the first in the proximal main vessel, a POT, the side branch rewired through a distal cell, a final kiss and a final POT. It leaves two layers of metal all round the proximal main vessel over the overlap and a double layer at the carina. It suits a side branch close in size to the distal main vessel, and both stents must expand to the proximal main vessel: manufacturer charts reproduced by the 18th EBC consensus give small-vessel designs an on-label maximum of 3.0 to 3.75 mm, and here the 2.5 mm stent opens to 3.75 mm. The side-branch wire comes out before the main-vessel stent and re-crosses through it afterwards, so no wire is left between the two layers (Frampton; the BBK 3 protocol; Toth 2020). In a DK-culotte the first opening toward the main vessel is a kiss instead of a single balloon.

      Sizing

      Each stent is sized 1:1 to the vessel distal to it: the main-vessel stent to the distal main vessel, the side-branch stent to the side branch. The proximal LAD is sized here by Finet's law, 0.678 × (distal main vessel + side branch), which agrees with measured bifurcations when the smaller daughter branch is at least 0.75 of the larger (Huo 2012; here 2.5 / 3.0 = 0.83) and holds for Y-shaped, not T-shaped, bifurcations (Ormiston 2018): 0.678 × (3.0 + 2.5) = 3.73, so the proximal LAD is 3.75 mm and every POT uses a 3.75 mm non-compliant balloon. Kissing balloons are short non-compliant balloons sized 1:1 to each branch with minimal overlap, inflated alone at high pressure, then together, and deflated together.

      About the model

      The bifurcation is an LAD and first diagonal: proximal LAD 3.75 mm, distal LAD 3.0 mm, diagonal 2.5 mm, and 50° between the distal LAD and the diagonal (CT and 3D-QCA series of LAD-diagonal bifurcations report 46 ± 19° and 48 ± 16°), with a lesion in each of the three segments (Medina 1,1,1). The fluoro view is LAO 15°, cranial 33°, close to the obtainable optimal LAD-diagonal view in 3D-QCA (14 ± 28° LAO, 33 ± 5° cranial; Tu 2012), and the coronary tree around it is a population-average model. The stent is a generic open-cell design, not any brand's: rings of eight crowns joined by three connectors. These are estimates: the plaque and how much of it remodels outward, the recoil after predilation, how far the carina shifts, the stent's cells and struts, where the balloon markers sit, and the dissection flap's size and place. The protrusions are measured along the LAD from the carina and chosen near the published ranges: 3.0 mm in DK crush (the EBC gives 2 to 3 mm), and 2.3 mm in mini-crush and TAP, the least that covers the diagonal's upper ostial edge at this angle, a little more than the 1 to 2 mm described for those two. Stents bend and open by geometry, not by a mechanical simulation. The layer colours and the numbers under each result are measured from the struts as drawn.
